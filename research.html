Like Parent, Like Child? The Intergenerational Transmission of Hand to Mouth Status

This paper studies the intergenerational persistence of hand to mouth (HtM) status, the mechanisms through which this status is transmitted across generations and its implications for inequality, consumption dynamics and policy. Using genealogical links in the PSID, I show that children of HtM parents are significantly more likely to be HtM themselves both in the extensive and intensive margin. This suggests that HtM status reflects persistent dynastic characteristics rather than only transitory shocks. To interpret these facts, I develop a dynastic heterogeneous agent incomplete markets model with bequests, discount factor heterogeneity, and intergenerational persistence in income. The model shows how inherited wealth, persistent earnings and saving behavior jointly reproduce the intergenerational persistence of HtM status. This implies that financial fragility and inequality are more persistent than in standard one generation environments.
