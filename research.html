Like Parent, Like Child? The Intergenerational Transmission of Hand to Mouth Status

Why do some households remain persistently close to the liquidity constraint while others accumulate financial buffers? Using genealogical links in the PSID, I document substantial intergenerational persistence in hand-to-mouth (HtM) status, with a parent--child correlation of 0.45 in lifetime HtM rates. This persistence reflects inherited wealth, persistent earnings capacity, and a component that family resources do not explain. I develop a dynastic heterogeneous-agent incomplete-markets model incorporating three corresponding channels of intergenerational transmission: inherited wealth, earnings capacity, and patience. Estimating the model by the simulated method of moments, I find that inherited wealth accounts for 61 percent of intergenerational HtM persistence, while inherited patience and earnings capacity account for 21 and 18 percent, respectively. These dynastic mechanisms also shape macroeconomic policy outcomes. Increasing unemployment-insurance generosity produces a larger steady-state welfare gain in the estimated economy than in a one-generation incomplete-markets benchmark. Finally, HtM households exhibit substantially larger consumption responses to aggregate productivity shocks.
