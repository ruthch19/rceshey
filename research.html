Like Parent, Like Child? The Intergenerational Transmission of Hand to Mouth Status

Why do some households remain persistently close to the liquidity margin while others accumulate substantial financial buffers? Using genealogical links in the PSID, I document that hand-to-mouth (HtM) exposure is strongly persistent across generations. The parent-child correlation in lifetime HtM exposure is approximately $0.45$, and a regression of the child's rate on the parent's falls from $0.43$ to $0.19$ under controls for parental wealth, income and demographics. Households saving behaviors and financial vulnerability is therefore partly inherited rather than solely the residue of transitory shocks. To account for this, I develop a dynastic heterogeneous-agent incomplete-markets model in which wealth, earnings capacity and patience are transmitted across generations, and estimate the strength of each channel by simulated method of moments. A counterfactual decomposition attributes $61$ percent of the intergenerational correlation to inherited wealth, with inherited patience and inherited earnings capacity accounting for about a fifth each. Dynastic transmission also changes what social insurance is worth. A conventional one-generation benchmark finds that more generous unemployment insurance reduces welfare, while the estimated model finds a gain. The reversal comes from the dynastic structure as a whole, not from discount-factor heterogeneity alone.
